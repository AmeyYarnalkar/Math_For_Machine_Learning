
## Step 1 — Start from the ONLY definition that matters

We already agreed on this truth:

$$
A v = \lambda v
$$

Meaning in plain English:

> Applying matrix **A** to vector **v** only scales it by **λ**

No rotation. Direction unchanged.

---

## Step 2 — Bring everything to one side (logic move)

We want to **compare both sides**.

$$
A v - \lambda v = 0
$$

Factor out **v**:

$$
(A - \lambda I)v = 0
$$

⚠️ **This step is CRITICAL**
This is where people blindly copy — but here’s what it means.

---

## Step 3 — Why introduce the Identity matrix (I)?

Because:

* $ \lambda v = \lambda I v $
* Identity matrix keeps vector unchanged

So subtraction is legal:

$
A - \lambda I
$

This new matrix tells us:

> “Which directions collapse to zero?”

---

## Step 4 — The key logic

$
(A - \lambda I)v = 0
$

This equation has **two possible cases**:

### Case 1 

$
v = 0
$

Not allowed
Eigenvectors **cannot be zero vectors**.

---

### Case 2 (THIS is what we want)

$
v \neq 0 \quad \text{and} \quad (A - \lambda I)v = 0
$

This means:

> A non-zero vector goes to zero
> → the matrix **crushes space**

That happens **ONLY when determinant = 0**.

---

## Step 5 — Why determinant = 0 (real meaning)

Determinant answers:

> Does this matrix squash space into lower dimension?

* det ≠ 0 → space preserved → only trivial solution
* det = 0 → space collapses → infinite non-zero solutions

We WANT infinite solutions → eigenvectors live there.

So:

$
\boxed{\det(A - \lambda I) = 0}
$

 **THIS is the eigenvalue equation**

Not magic.
Pure linear algebra logic.

---

## Step 6 — This equation gives eigenvalues

Solve:

$
\det(A - \lambda I) = 0
$

→ you get values of **λ**

These λ are your **eigenvalues**.

---

## Step 7 — Then eigenvectors come naturally

For each eigenvalue λ:

1. Plug λ back into:
   $
   (A - \lambda I)v = 0
   $
2. Solve the system
3. Any non-zero solution is an **eigenvector**

---

## Step 8 — Let’s do a FULL 2×2 example (slow and clean)

Matrix:
$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
4 & 1 \\
2 & 3
\end{bmatrix}
$

---

### Step 8.1 — Form $A - \lambda I$

$$
A - \lambda I =
\begin{bmatrix}
4-\lambda & 1 \\
2 & 3-\lambda
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

### Step 8.2 — Take determinant

$$
\det(A - \lambda I)
= (4-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - 2
$$

Expand:

$$
= \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10
$$

---

### Step 8.3 — Solve characteristic equation

$$
\lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0
$$

$$
(\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 2) = 0
$$

Eigenvalues:
$$
\boxed{\lambda = 5,; 2}
$$

---

## Step 9 — Find eigenvectors (THIS is easy now)

### For λ = 5

$$
(A - 5I)v = 0
\Rightarrow
\begin{bmatrix}
-1 & 1 \\
2 & -2
\end{bmatrix} v = 0
$$

Solve:
$$
x = y
$$

Eigenvector:

$$\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

### For λ = 2

$$
(A - 2I)v = 0
\Rightarrow
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
2 & 1
\end{bmatrix} v = 0
$$

Solve:
$
2x + y = 0 \Rightarrow y = -2x
$

Eigenvector:
$
\begin{bmatrix}1\\-2\end{bmatrix}
$

---


## Step 10 — Final mental model (don’t forget this)

* Matrix = transformation
* Eigenvector = direction that survives
* Eigenvalue = how hard it gets stretched
* Determinant = volume scaling
* det(A − λI) = 0 → space collapses → eigenvectors appear

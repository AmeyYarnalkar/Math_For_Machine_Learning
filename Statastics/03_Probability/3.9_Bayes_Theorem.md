

#  Bayes’ Theorem 

## Independent vs Dependent Events

### Independent Events

Two events are **independent** if one does NOT affect the other.

Mathematically:

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(A),P(B)
$$

Meaning:

👉 Knowing A happened gives zero information about B.

### Example

* Tossing two coins
* Rain in Delhi vs traffic in Tokyo

---

### Dependent Events

Events are **dependent** if one event changes the probability of the other.

So:

$$
P(A \cap B) \neq P(A)P(B)
$$

Example:

* Having a disease and testing positive
* Studying and exam results

Most real-world problems (ML included) involve **dependent events**.

---

## Conditional Probability 

This is the real foundation of Bayes.

👉 Conditional probability means:

> “Probability of A **given** B has already happened.”

Notation:

$$
P(A|B)
$$

Formula:

$$
P(A|B)=\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

${P(A \cap B)}$ means probability of A and B

Interpretation:

* We shrink our universe to cases where B happened.
* Then ask: how often did A also happen?

---

#  Key Identity 

From conditional probability:

$$
P(A \cap B)=P(A|B)P(B)
$$

Similarly:

$$
P(A \cap B)=P(B|A)P(A)
$$

Since both equal the same joint probability:

$$
P(A|B)P(B)=P(B|A)P(A)
$$

This single equality leads directly to Bayes’ theorem.

---

## Bayes’ Theorem — Derivation

Start with:

$$
P(A|B)P(B)=P(B|A)P(A)
$$

Divide both sides by (P(B)):

$$
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}
$$

Boom. That’s Bayes.

---

# Meaning of Each Term 

$$
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}
$$

Think of it like updating belief.

* **P(A)** → Prior
  What you believed before seeing evidence.

* **P(B|A)** → Likelihood
  How likely evidence is if A is true.

* **P(B)** → Evidence (normalization)
  Total probability of observing B.

* **P(A|B)** → Posterior
  Updated belief after seeing B.

---

## Why Bayes Exists 

Normal conditional probability asks:

👉 “What is chance of B if A happened?”

But real problems ask:

👉 “Given that I observed B… what is chance A is true?”

Example:

* Test says positive.
* You don’t want P(test|disease).
* You want P(disease|test).

Bayes flips the direction.

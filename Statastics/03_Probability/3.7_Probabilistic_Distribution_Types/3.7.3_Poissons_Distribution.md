## The problem Poisson is trying to solve

Binomial answers this question:

> “Out of **n fixed trials**, how many successes happen?”

Poisson answers a **different** question:

> “How many times does an event happen in a **fixed time or space**,
> when events happen **randomly**?”

Key shift:

* No fixed number of trials
* We are counting **events**

---

## Real-life situations Poisson was made for

Think like this:

* Number of **calls** to a call center in 1 hour
* Number of **customers** entering a shop in 10 minutes
* Number of **accidents** at a junction in a day
* Number of **typos** per page in a book

You are not doing trials.
You are **waiting and counting**.

---

## Core assumptions 

Poisson works **only if all are true**:

1. Events occur **randomly**
2. Events are **independent**
3. Events happen **one at a time**
4. Average rate is **constant**
5. Two events don’t happen at the exact same instant

---

## The ONLY parameter: λ (lambda)

Poisson has **one parameter**:

$$
\lambda = \text{average number of events}
$$

Example:

* Average emails per hour = 5
  → $ \lambda = 5 $

---

## What Poisson random variable represents

Let:
$$
X = \text{number of events in a fixed interval}
$$

Then:
$$
X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)
$$

Possible values:

```
0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
```

No upper bound.

---

## Poisson probability formula (don’t panic)

$$
\boxed{
P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k}{k!}
}
$$


---

## Meaning of each term (this is the intuition)

### $ \lambda^k $

* Favors outcomes near the average

### $  k! $

* Removes ordering (same logic as Binomial)
* Because when you consider an order same logic get counted again and again
* Events are unordered

### $ e^{-\lambda} $

* Ensures total probability = 1


---

## Simple numeric example

Average calls per hour = 2
So:
$
\lambda = 2
$

What’s the probability of **exactly 3 calls** in an hour?

$$
P(X=3) = \frac{e^{-2} 2^3}{3!}
$$

$$
= \frac{e^{-2} \cdot 8}{6}
\approx 0.18
$$

---

## Mean, Variance, Std Deviation (super important)

This is where Poisson is **beautifully simple**:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Mean} = \text{Variance} = \lambda
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Std Dev} = \sqrt{\lambda}
}
$$

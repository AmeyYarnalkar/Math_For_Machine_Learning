
# Measures of Central Tendency & Spread

## (For Probabilistic Distributions)

When working with **probabilistic distributions**, we **do not have data points**.
We only know:

* possible values of a random variable
* how likely those values are

So every measure is defined using **probability**, not counting.

---

## Mean (Expected Value)

### What it represents

> The long-run average value you would get if the experiment were repeated many times.

---

### How it is calculated (Discrete)

$$
\mu = \mathbb{E}[X] = \sum x \cdot P(X = x)
$$

* Each value is **weighted by its probability**
* This replaces the arithmetic average used in raw data

---

### How it is calculated (Continuous)

$$
\mu = \mathbb{E}[X] = \int x , f(x), dx
$$

* Probability density replaces probability
* Area weighting replaces frequency

---

### Why this definition makes sense

* Values that occur more often should influence the mean more
* Probability replaces frequency

## Mode

### What it represents

> The most likely outcome.

---

### How it is calculated

* **Discrete (PMF)** → value with highest probability
  $$
  \text{Mode} = \arg\max_x P(X=x)
  $$

* **Continuous (PDF)** → value where density is maximum
  $$
  \text{Mode} = \arg\max_x f(x)
  $$

---

### Interpretation

* Most frequent (data)
* Most probable (distribution)
* Most dense (continuous case)

---

## Variance

### What it represents

> How much values typically deviate from the mean.

---

### How it is calculated (Core definition)

$$
\text{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\big[(X - \mu)^2\big]
$$

* Squaring avoids cancellation
* Measures **spread around the mean**

---

### Equivalent form (often used)

$$
\text{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2
$$

Derived from the main definition.

---

## Standard Deviation

### What it represents

> Typical distance of values from the mean, in original units.

---

### How it is calculated

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}
$$

---

### Why it exists

* Variance is in squared units
* Standard deviation restores original scale

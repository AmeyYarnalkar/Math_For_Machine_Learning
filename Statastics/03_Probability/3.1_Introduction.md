

# What is Probability? 

**Probability = how likely something is to happen**

* Scale: **0 to 1**

  * `0` → impossible
  * `1` → guaranteed

### Mathematical definition

$$
P(\text{Event}) = \frac{\text{Favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total possible outcomes}}
$$

**Example**

* Toss a coin
* Outcomes = {H, T} → 2
* Probability of Head:
  $$
  P(H) = \frac{1}{2}
  $$

---

#  What is an Event?

**Event = a specific outcome or group of outcomes you care about**

* Sample Space (S): all possible outcomes
* Event (E): subset of S

**Example**

* Roll a die
* S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}
* Event E = “Even number” = {2,4,6}

---

# Why Do We Add Probabilities?

### 👉 **Addition answers this question:**

> “What is the chance that **at least one** of these events happens?”

So whenever you see **OR**, think **ADDITION**.

---

# Mutually Exclusive Events

### Meaning (very important)

**Mutually exclusive = cannot happen at the same time**

If one happens, the other **cannot**.

**Example**

* One coin toss:

  * Head OR Tail
  * You cannot get both

### Mathematical property

$$
P(A \cap B) = 0
$$

### Additive rule (simple)

$$
P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)
$$

**Example**

* Die roll
* A = getting 2 → (1/6)
* B = getting 5 → (1/6)

$$
P(A \text{ or } B) = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{6}
$$

---

# Non-Mutually Exclusive Events

### Meaning

**They CAN happen together**

**Example**

* Card draw:

  * A = “Card is red”
  * B = “Card is a king”
  * Red king exists → overlap

### Why simple addition FAILS

Because overlap gets **counted twice**

### Correct Additive Rule

$$
P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)
$$

### Example

* Deck of 52 cards
* Red cards = 26
* Kings = 4
* Red kings = 2

$$
P(A \cup B) = \frac{26}{52} + \frac{4}{52} - \frac{2}{52}
$$

---

# What Does Addition Really Give Us?

### Addition tells us:

> Probability that **at least one event occurs**

**Key idea**

* Used when outcomes are **choices / alternatives**
* Language clue: **OR**

---

# Why Do We Multiply Probabilities?

### 👉 **Multiplication answers this question:**

> “What is the chance that **both events happen together**?”

Whenever you see **AND**, think **MULTIPLICATION**.

---

# Independent Events

### Meaning

**One event does NOT affect the other**

**Example**

* Tossing two coins
* First toss doesn’t affect second

### Multiplicative Rule

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)
$$

### Example

* P(Head) = 1/2
* Two heads:
  $$
  \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}
  $$

---

# Dependent Events

### Meaning

**One event DOES affect the other**

Usually happens **without replacement**

**Example**

* Drawing cards without replacement

### Formula

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B \mid A)
$$

Where:
$$
P(B \mid A) = \text{Probability of B AFTER A happened}
$$

### Example

* Draw 2 red cards from deck without replacement

$$
P(\text{1st red}) = \frac{26}{52}
$$
$$
P(\text{2nd red} \mid \text{1st red}) = \frac{25}{51}
$$

$$
P = \frac{26}{52} \times \frac{25}{51}
$$

---

# What Does Multiplication Really Give Us?

### Multiplication tells us:

> Probability that **all events happen together**

**Key idea**

* Used when events occur **in sequence**
* Language clue: **AND**

---

# Why “Mutually Exclusive” Is NOT Used in Multiplication

### Mutually exclusive means:

$$
P(A \cap B) = 0
$$

### Multiplication calculates:

$
P(A \cap B)
$

So if events are mutually exclusive:

* They **cannot happen together**
* Multiplication result is **zero**
* Hence, the concept is **meaningless for multiplication**

👉 **Mutual exclusivity is about OR (addition), not AND (multiplication)**

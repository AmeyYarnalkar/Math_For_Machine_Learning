A **probabilistic distribution** depends on:

* **Input (x)** → values of the random variable
* **Output (y)** → what we report about probability

Now we are asking:

> **Based on how the OUTPUT behaves or is defined, what different distributions/functions do we have?**

---

## Big Picture 

> Based on **output variation**, distributions differ in **what the output represents**:
> probability, density, cumulative probability, or tail probability.

Now let’s list them **cleanly and structurally**.

---

## Classification Based on OUTPUT VARIATION

### Probability at a value → **PMF**

**Output meaning**

* Output = **exact probability of a value**

$$
y = P(X = x)
$$

**Applies to**

* Discrete inputs

**Output behavior**

* Values between 0 and 1
* Sum of all outputs = 1

**Interpretation**

* “How likely is this exact value?”

---

### Probability density → **PDF**

**Output meaning**

* Output = **density**, not probability

$$
y = f(x)
$$

**Applies to**

* Continuous inputs

**Output behavior**

* Can be greater than 1
* Does NOT sum to 1
* Probability comes from **area**, not height

**Interpretation**

* “How densely probability is packed around this value?”

---

### Cumulative probability → **CDF**

**Output meaning**

* Output = probability **up to** a value

$$
y = P(X \le x)
$$

**Applies to**

* Both discrete and continuous inputs

**Output behavior**

* Always between 0 and 1
* Always non-decreasing
* Ends at 1

**Interpretation**

* “What fraction of outcomes lie to the left of x?”

**Key connection**

* Slope of CDF = PDF (for continuous case)

---

### Tail probability → **Complementary CDF (CCDF)**

**Output meaning**

* Output = probability **beyond** a value

$$
y = P(X > x)
$$

**Relation**
$$
\text{CCDF} = 1 - \text{CDF}
$$

**Interpretation**

* “How likely is it to exceed x?”
* Used heavily in:

  * risk
  * reliability
  * extreme events

---

### Joint probability → **Joint Distribution**

**Output meaning**

* Probability of **multiple variables together**

$$
P(X = x, Y = y)
$$

or density:
$$
f(x, y)
$$

**Interpretation**

* “How do two variables behave together?”

This leads to:

* covariance
* correlation
* dependence

---

### Conditional probability → **Conditional Distribution**

**Output meaning**

* Probability of one variable **given** another

$$
P(X \mid Y)
$$

**Interpretation**

* “How does probability change when information is known?”

This is the backbone of:

* Bayes theorem
* ML models
* inference

---

## Summary Table 

| Output represents                 | Distribution / Function  |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Probability at a value            | PMF                      |
| Probability density               | PDF                      |
| Probability up to a value         | CDF                      |
| Probability beyond a value        | CCDF                     |
| Probability of multiple variables | Joint distribution       |
| Probability given information     | Conditional distribution |

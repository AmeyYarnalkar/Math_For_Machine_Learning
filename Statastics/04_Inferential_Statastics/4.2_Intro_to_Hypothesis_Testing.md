# Hypothesis testing

## What it **actually** means

Hypothesis testing is just a **decision-making tool under uncertainty**.

## 1. Null Hypothesis

This is the starting assumption.
It says nothing unusual is happening.

Examples:

* The drug has no effect.
* The update did not improve anything.
* The average height is 170 cm.

We begin by treating this as true.
we keep a conservative mindset at first.

---

## 2. Alternative Hypothesis

This is the opposite idea.
It represents what you think might actually be happening.

Examples:

* The drug works.
* The update improved results.
* The average height is different from 170 cm.

---

## 3. Statistical Test

You take a sample and look at the result.

Then you ask:
“If the starting assumption were true, would this result look normal or very unusual?”

* If the result looks normal → keep the starting assumption.
* If the result looks very unusual → reject the starting assumption.

Example:
A coin claims to be fair.
You flip it 100 times and get 80 heads.
That looks very unusual for a fair coin, so you reject the claim that it is fair.

You are not proving the opposite is 100% true — you are only saying the original assumption does not fit the data well.

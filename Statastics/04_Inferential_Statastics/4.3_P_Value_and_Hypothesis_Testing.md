# p-value 

## Where p-value comes from

* In hypothesis testing, we first define:

  * Null Hypothesis
  * Alternative Hypothesis
* Then we perform a **statistical test** on sample data.
* The statistical test produces a number.
* That number is called the **p-value**.

So:

statistical analysis → gives a result → that result = p-value

---

## What p-value tells us

* It shows how surprising the sample result is if the starting assumption were true, for example, getting 9 heads in 10 coin flips when we expect 5.
* It is only a **measure of unusualness**, not proof of anything.

---

## How to read it

* Large p-value → result looks normal → keep the starting assumption.
* Small p-value → result looks strange → reject the starting assumption.

---

## Important clarity

* p-value is NOT the whole statistical test.
* p-value is the **output** of the statistical test.
* It is just a single number used to help make a decision.

---

## Simple Example

* Null Hypothesis: the coin is fair.
* You flip the coin 10 times and get 9 heads.
* A statistical test is applied to this result and it outputs a p-value.
* If the p-value is very small → getting 9 heads looks unusual → reject the fair coin idea.
* If the p-value is large → the result still looks normal → keep the fair coin idea.

---

## One-line summary

p-value = the final number produced by statistical analysis that helps decide whether the starting assumption should be rejected.

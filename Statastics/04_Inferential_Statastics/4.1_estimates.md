# Estimates

In very simple terms, an estimate in statistics is a smart guess about a whole group based on a small part of it.

Since we usually can’t check everyone or everything, we take a small sample and use it to guess the true value for the entire population.

## Simple Example 1: Average Height

Imagine a college has 10,000 students.
You want to know the average height of all students.

You can’t measure all 10,000. So you measure 100 students.

Suppose the average height of those 100 students is 170 cm.

👉 That 170 cm is your estimate of the average height of all 10,000 students.

It may not be perfectly exact, but it’s a reasonable guess.

## Simple Example 2: Election Poll

A country has 1 crore voters.
A news agency asks 1,000 people who they will vote for.

If 600 out of 1,000 say Party A, that means:

$$ 600 / 1000
=
60 \%
$$

👉 So they estimate that around 60% of the whole country supports Party A.

Again, it’s a guess based on a smaller group.

## In One Line

**An estimate = value calculated from a sample to guess the true population value.**


## Parameter vs Estimate

Parameter → The true value of the whole population (usually unknown).

Estimate → The value we calculate from the sample.

Example
Suppose:

True average height of all students = 172 cm
👉 That 172 cm is the parameter (but we don’t know it).


You measure 100 students and get:


170 cm


👉 That 170 cm is your estimate.
So:


**Parameter = Truth (unknown)**


**Estimate = Our guess (from sample)**


## What is an Estimator?

>An estimator is the formula or rule we use to calculate the estimate.

Example:
To estimate population mean.
When you plug in numbers and get 170 cm,
that 170 is the estimate.
So:


Estimator → formula

Estimate → final number



## Point Estimate vs Interval Estimate
### Point Estimate
A single number.\
Example:
Average height ≈ 170 cm
Just one value.

### Interval Estimate
A range of values.\
Example:
Average height is between 168 cm and 172 cm.
This is better because it shows uncertainty.
In real life, interval estimates are more reliable.

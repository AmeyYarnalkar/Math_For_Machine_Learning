#  ANOVA 

##  What is ANOVA?

**ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)** is a statistical test used to:

> Determine whether the means of 3 or more groups are significantly different.

Instead of comparing means directly, ANOVA compares **variances**.

It calculates:

$$
F = \frac{\text{Between-group variance}}{\text{Within-group variance}}
$$

If F is large → at least one group mean is different.
If F ≈ 1 → group means are similar.

---

## Components (Factors & Levels)

###  Factor

A factor is the **independent variable** (categorical variable).

Example:

* Teaching Method
* City
* Gender

---

###  Levels (Labels)

Levels are the **categories inside a factor**.

Example:

Factor = Teaching Method
Levels = Method A, Method B, Method C

Factor = City
Levels = Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore

So:

👉 Factor = variable
👉 Levels = categories of that variable

---

## Core Components of ANOVA Model

ANOVA mainly works with:

1. Between-group variation
2. Within-group variation
3. F-ratio

---

###  Between-Group Variation

Measures how much group means differ from overall mean.

If group averages are far apart → this value is large.

---

### Within-Group Variation

Measures how much values vary inside each group.

If data inside groups is tightly packed → this value is small.

---

###  F-Ratio

$$
F = \frac{\text{Between variance}}{\text{Within variance}}
$$

If:

* F large → reject H₀
* F small → fail to reject H₀

---

## Assumptions of ANOVA


### 1. Normality

Data **within each group** should be approximately normally distributed.

Important correction:
It is not “sample means must be normal.”
It is the data inside each group that should be roughly normal.

---

### 2. Independence

Observations must be independent.

* One observation should not influence another.
* Data should be randomly selected.

---

### 3️. Homogeneity of Variance

All groups must have approximately equal variance.

If one group has very high spread and another very low spread, ANOVA results may be unreliable.

---

### 4. No Extreme Outliers

Extreme outliers can distort mean and variance,
which directly affects the F-ratio.

---

## Types of ANOVA


##  One-Way ANOVA

* One factor
* Two or more independent levels
* Groups are independent

Example:
Compare marks across 3 teaching methods.

---

##  Repeated Measures ANOVA

* One factor
* Same subjects measured multiple times
* Observations are dependent

Example:
Student scores before and after training.

---

##  Factorial ANOVA (Two-Way or More)

* Two or more factors
* Can test:

  * Main effects
  * Interaction effects

Example:
Effect of Teaching Method and Gender on marks.

---

## Important Clarification

In factorial ANOVA:

Factors can be independent,
but interaction between them is tested.

It’s not about “levels being dependent or independent,”
it’s about how factors combine to influence the dependent variable.

# ONE-WAY ANOVA –  Hypothesis Testing


## STEP 1️: State the Hypotheses

Suppose we have 3 groups.

H₀ (Null Hypothesis):

$$
\mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3
$$

(All group means are equal)

H₁ (Alternative Hypothesis):

At least one group mean is different.

---

## STEP 2️: Understand the Model Structure

ANOVA splits total variation into two parts:

$$
\text{Total Variation} = \text{Between-group Variation} + \text{Within-group Variation}
$$

In symbols:

$$
SST = SSB + SSW
$$

Where:

* SST = Total Sum of Squares
* SSB = Sum of Squares Between
* SSW = Sum of Squares Within

---

## STEP 3️: Compute Total Sum of Squares (SST)

$$
SST = \sum (X_{ij} - \bar{X})^2
$$

Where:

* $ X_{ij} $ = individual observation
* $ \bar{X} $ = overall mean

Meaning:

Total variability of all data from overall mean.

---

## STEP 4️: Compute Between-Group Sum of Squares (SSB)

$$
SSB = \sum n_i (\bar{X}_i - \bar{X})^2
$$

Where:

* $ n_i $ = size of group i
* $ \bar{X}_i $ = mean of group i
* $ \bar{X} $ = overall mean

Meaning:

Measures how far each group mean is from overall mean.

If group means are very different → SSB large.

---

## STEP 5️: Compute Within-Group Sum of Squares (SSW)

$$
SSW = \sum \sum (X_{ij} - \bar{X}_i)^2
$$

Meaning:

Measures variation inside each group.

If data inside groups is scattered → SSW large.

---

## STEP 6️: Degrees of Freedom

Between groups:

$$
df_1 = k - 1
$$

Within groups:

$$
df_2 = N - k
$$

Where:

* k = number of groups
* N = total observations

---

## STEP 7️: Compute Mean Squares

We convert sums of squares into variances.

Between-group Mean Square:

$$
MSB = \frac{SSB}{k - 1}
$$

Within-group Mean Square:

$$
MSW = \frac{SSW}{N - k}
$$

---

## STEP 8️: Compute F-Statistic

$$
F = \frac{MSB}{MSW}
$$

Interpretation:

* If MSB ≈ MSW → F ≈ 1
* If MSB >> MSW → F large

Large F → reject H₀

---

## STEP 9️: Compare with Critical Value

We use F-distribution.

Find critical value using:

* df₁ = k − 1
* df₂ = N − k
* significance level (α, usually 0.05)

Decision rule:

If:
$$
F > F_{\text{critical}}
$$
→ Reject H₀

Otherwise:
Fail to reject H₀

---

## What Each Formula Means (Conceptually)

SST → Total variability in data
SSB → Variability due to group differences
SSW → Variability due to randomness inside groups

F → Signal / Noise ratio

Signal = Between-group variation
Noise = Within-group variation

If signal > noise → significant difference.

---

## Complete Flow Summary

1. State H₀ and H₁
2. Compute overall mean
3. Compute SSB
4. Compute SSW
5. Compute MSB and MSW
6. Compute F
7. Compare with critical value
8. Conclude

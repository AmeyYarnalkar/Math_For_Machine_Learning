

#  CHI-SQUARE TEST 


##   What is Chi-Square Test?

Chi-square test is used to determine:

> Whether observed categorical data differs significantly from expected data.

It works only on **categorical data** and their **counts**.

---

## When Do We Use It?

There are two main uses:

### A) Goodness of Fit

Checks whether data follows a claimed distribution.

Example:

* Is a die fair?
* Does customer preference follow company claim?

---

### B) Test of Independence (Most important for ML)

Checks whether two categorical variables are related.

Example:

* Gender vs Purchase
* Education vs Income category
* Feature vs Target variable

Used in ML for feature selection.

---

## Key Terminology

### Observed (O)

Actual count from data.

### Expected (E)

Count expected if null hypothesis is true.

---

## Null Hypothesis (H₀)

Goodness of fit:
Data follows expected distribution.

Independence:
Variables are independent (no relationship).

---

## Core Formula

$$
\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}
$$

Where:

* O = Observed
* E = Expected

---

### Why square?

To avoid positive and negative differences canceling.

### Why divide by E?

To measure relative deviation, not raw difference.

---

## Expected Frequency Calculation

### For Goodness of Fit

$$
E = \text{Total} \times \text{Theoretical Probability}
$$

---

### For Independence Test

$$
E = \frac{(\text{Row Total}) \times (\text{Column Total})}{\text{Grand Total}}
$$

---

## Degrees of Freedom (df)

Goodness of Fit:

$$
df = \text{number of categories} - 1
$$

Independence Test:

$$
df = (r - 1)(c - 1)
$$

Reason:
One value is fixed due to total constraint.

---

## Chi-Square Distribution

![Image](https://di-mgt.com.au/images/chisquare-pvalue.gif)



Properties:

* Always ≥ 0
* Right-skewed
* Shape depends on degrees of freedom
* Large values are rare

Right tail = rejection region.

---

## Decision Rule

Compare computed χ² value with critical value.

If:
χ² > critical value
→ Reject H₀

If:
χ² ≤ critical value
→ Fail to reject H₀

Critical value depends on:

* Degrees of freedom
* Significance level (α)

---

## Assumptions

1. Data is categorical.
2. Observations are independent.
3. Expected frequency in each cell ≥ 5 (important rule).

---

## Interpretation Summary

Small χ² value:
Observed ≈ Expected
Difference likely due to chance.

Large χ² value:
Observed far from Expected
Difference unlikely due to chance.

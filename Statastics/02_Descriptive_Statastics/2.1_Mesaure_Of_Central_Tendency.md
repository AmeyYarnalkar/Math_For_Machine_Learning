

# Measure of Central Tendency

A **measure of central tendency** is a statistical metric that
**describes the central or representative value** of a dataset.

👉 It helps answer:
**“What is a typical value in this data?”**

---

## Main Measures of Central Tendency

There are **three** commonly used measures:

1. **Mean (Average)**
2. **Median**
3. **Mode**

---

## Mean (Average)

### Definition

Mean is calculated as:

> **Sum of all values ÷ total number of values**

---

### Types of Mean

#### Population Mean (μ)

Used when **entire population data** is available.

$$
\mu = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i}{N}
$$

* $N$ → population size
* $x_i$ → individual data points

---

#### Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$)

Used when working with a **sample**.

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i}{n}
$$

* $n$ → sample size

👉 In ML, we almost always deal with **sample mean**, not population mean.

---

### Characteristics of Mean

* **Highly affected by extreme outliers**
* Best represents data when distribution is **symmetric**
* Works only for:

  * **Interval scale**
  * **Ratio scale**

Mean has **no built-in resistance** to extreme values
→ One bad value can distort it badly

---

## Median

### Definition

Median is the **middle value** when the dataset is **sorted**
in ascending or descending order.

---

### How to Find Median

1. Sort the data
2. Count total number of elements

* **If odd (n):**
  $$
  \text{Median} = \text{value at position } \frac{n+1}{2}
  $$

* **If even (n):**
  $$
  \text{Median} = \frac{\text{middle value} + \text{next middle value}}{2}
  $$

---

### Characteristics of Median

* **Not affected by extreme outliers**
* Robust measure for **skewed data**
* Can be used for:

  * **Ordinal**
  * **Interval**
  * **Ratio**

👉 This is why median is preferred for:

* Income data
* House prices
* Any real-world uneven distributions

---

## Mode

### Definition

Mode is the **most frequently occurring value** in the dataset.

---

### Characteristics of Mode

* **Not affected by outliers**
* Can be used for **all four measurement scales**
* Only measure usable for:

  * **Nominal data**

Examples:

* Most common blood group
* Most frequent product sold

⚠️ A dataset can have:

* No mode
* One mode
* Multiple modes

---

## Choosing the Right Measure (Very Important)

### Choose **Mean**

* Data is **symmetrically distributed**
* No extreme outliers
* Numerical (interval or ratio)

---

### Choose **Median**

* Data is **skewed**
* Outliers are present
* Real-world economic or social data

---

### Choose **Mode**

* Data is **categorical**
* Nominal scale
* Frequency matters more than magnitude

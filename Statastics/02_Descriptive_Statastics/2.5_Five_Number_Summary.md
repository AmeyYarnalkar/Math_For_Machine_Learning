
# Five-Number Summary

The **five-number summary** is a compact statistical description
of a dataset’s **center, spread, and extremes**.

It consists of:

1. **Minimum value**
2. **First Quartile (Q1)**
3. **Median (Q2)**
4. **Third Quartile (Q3)**
5. **Maximum value**

👉 Together, these five values give a **robust snapshot** of the data distribution.

---

## Why the Five-Number Summary Matters

* Works even when data is **skewed**
* Less affected by **outliers** than mean/variance
* Foundation of **box plots**
* Widely used in **EDA and ML preprocessing**

---

## Interquartile Range (IQR)

Before detecting outliers, compute:

$$
\text{IQR} = Q3 - Q1
$$

* Represents the spread of the **middle 50%** of the data
* Robust to extreme values

---

## Outlier Detection Using Fences

Outliers are identified using **lower and upper fences**.

### Lower Fence

$$
\text{Lower Fence} = Q1 - 1.5 \times IQR
$$

### Upper Fence

$$
\text{Upper Fence} = Q3 + 1.5 \times IQR
$$

Any value:

* **Below lower fence**
* **Above upper fence**

→ considered an **outlier**

---

## Correct Step-by-Step Process (Important)

1. **Sort the data**
2. Compute:

   * Q1
   * Median (Q2)
   * Q3
3. Compute:

   * IQR = Q3 − Q1
4. Define:

   * Lower fence
   * Upper fence
5. Identify outliers
6. Determine:

   * Minimum (excluding outliers)
   * Maximum (excluding outliers)

---

## Important Correction 

>  “Median should be calculated after removing outliers”

This is **not statistically correct**.

### Reality:

* **Median is already robust**
* Outliers **do not affect the median**
* Median should be computed on the **original sorted data**

What *does* change after removing outliers:

* **Minimum**
* **Maximum**
* Possibly **Q1 and Q3** (only if outliers are extreme and sample is small)

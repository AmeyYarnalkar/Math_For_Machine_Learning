

# Slope, Tangent, and Instantaneous Rate of Change 

## What is Slope?


![Image](https://study.com/cimages/multimages/16/rise_run_ex1_49042396506153420869.jpg)


Slope describes **how steep a line is** and how one variable changes relative to another.

### Three Practical Interpretations

**Steepness of a line**
How tilted the line is.

**Rate of change**
How fast (y) changes when (x) changes.

**Rise over Run**
$$
\text{Slope} = \frac{\text{Vertical Change}}{\text{Horizontal Change}}
$$

---

###  Slope Formula

For two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$:

$$
m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

👉 Careful: numerator is **change in y**, denominator is **change in x**.

---

###  Constant Rate of Change

For a straight line:

* The slope is **constant everywhere**.
* That’s why linear graphs look uniform.

---

##  Types of Slopes

> Slope is represented by the letter *$m$*

###  Positive Slope

* (m > 0)
* Line rises left → right
* Larger (m) = steeper upward.

###  Negative Slope

* (m < 0)
* Line falls left → right.
* More negative = steeper downward.

###  Zero (Neutral) Slope

* (m = 0)
* Horizontal line.
* No change in (y).

### Undefined Slope

* $x_2 = x_1$
* Vertical line.
* Division by zero ⇒ slope undefined (often called infinite).

---

## 3. Why Does (y = mx + c) Make Sense?

This is the equation of a straight line.

$$
y = mx + c
$$

Where:

* $m$ → slope $rate of change)
* $c$ → interc$p$ (starting value when (x=0))

### Intuition

Think of it like:

👉 Start at height (c).
👉 Every time (x) increases by 1, add (m).

So the line grows linearly because the rate of change is constant.

---

## What Happens When the Graph is a Curve?


![Image](https://www.mathsisfun.com/geometry/images/secant-line.svg)


In curves:

* Slope is **not constant**.
* At every point, the steepness changes.

So we ask:

👉 “What is the slope at *this exact point*?”

This leads to the idea of **instantaneous slope**.

---

##  Secant Line vs Tangent Line

###  Secant Line

Take two points on the curve:

$$
(x, f(x)), \quad (x+h, f(x+h))
$$

Slope of secant:

$$
\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
$$

This gives **average rate of change**.

---

###  Tangent Line

Now imagine:

* The second point moves closer.
* $h \rightarrow 0$.

The secant line becomes a line touching at only one point.

That line is the **tangent**.

👉 Tangent slope = instantaneous rate of change.

---

##  Instantaneous Slope (Derivative)

The exact slope at a point:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}
$$

Defined using limits:

$$
\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
$$

### What this really means

You are shrinking the gap between two points until they overlap.

Average change → Exact change.

That’s the core idea of calculus.

##  7. Conceptual Flow (Big Picture)

Straight Line:

* One slope.
* Equation (y = mx + c).

Curve:

* Infinite slopes.
* Need tangent.
* Tangent slope = derivative.

---

## Quick  Summary

* Slope = how fast (y) changes wrt (x).
* Linear graph → constant slope.
* Curve → changing slope.
* Secant = two-point average slope.
* Tangent = one-point exact slope.
* Derivative = limit of secant slope.

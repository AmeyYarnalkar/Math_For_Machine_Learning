## Derivatives of Trigonometric, Logarithmic & Exponential Functions


## 1️. Trigonometric Function Derivatives

## Basic Six 

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\cos x
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)=-\sin x
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x)=\sec^2 x
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x)=-\csc^2 x
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x)=\sec x \tan x
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\csc x)=-\csc x \cot x
$$

---

## Intuition Pattern 

* **sin → cos**
* **cos → -sin** (cos always introduces minus)
* **tan / sec** go together
* **cot / csc** go together and usually negative

---

## Example

If
$$
f(x)=3\sin x - x^2
$$

Derivative:

$$
f'(x)=3\cos x - 2x
$$

Just apply rules term-by-term.

## 2️. Exponential Function Derivatives

## Base (e) — Most Important

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(e^x)=e^x
$$

This function is special because **its slope equals itself**.

---

## General Exponential

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(a^x)=a^x \ln a
$$

where (a>0).

---

## Examples

### Example 1

$$
f(x)=e^x
$$
Derivative:
$$
f'(x)=e^x
$$

---

### Example 2

$$
f(x)=2^x
$$
Derivative:
$$
f'(x)=2^x \ln 2
$$

---

## Intuition

Exponential growth means:

* Growth rate proportional to current value.
* That’s why (e^x) stays the same after differentiation.

This shows up everywhere in ML — gradients, probability, softmax, etc.

---

## 3️. Logarithmic Function Derivatives

## Natural Log (Most Important)

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x)=\frac{1}{x}
$$

---

## Log with Base (a)

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\log_a x)=\frac{1}{x\ln a}
$$

---

## Examples

### Example 1

$$
f(x)=\ln x
$$
Derivative:
$$
f'(x)=\frac{1}{x}
$$

---

### Example 2

$$
f(x)=5\ln x
$$
Derivative:
$$
f'(x)=\frac{5}{x}
$$

---

## Intuition

Log grows slower and slower → slope decreases as (x) increases.
That’s why derivative is (1/x).
